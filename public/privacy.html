What your office shares.

tinyAGENTS is a public beta operated from the michaelraim/tinyagents project. Offices are private by default even though anyone can create one.

GitHub and GitLab sign-in

Social login stores your provider account ID, display name, email, profile-image URL, linked-provider records and login sessions in Cloudflare D1. Provider tokens are encrypted with the application secret. Sessions may include IP address and browser user-agent information. We request profile and email access only, never repository access. GitLab.com is supported; Google is not used. Your account owns one office. Linking a second provider is explicit, not an automatic email-based merge.

Signed-in owners can manage sharing, the office clock, computer connections and deletion. Session cookies are HttpOnly, Secure on the hosted site and SameSite=Lax so provider callbacks work. Sessions expire after 30 days; signing out revokes that session. Open account streams check session validity on updates and heartbeats.

Connecting a computer

On first trusted use, the plugin sends its computer name, coding client and time zone to request a browser connection. Activity reporting begins only after you approve. Requests expire after 15 minutes and are removed when a later request is created. The browser link cannot retrieve the reporting credential: that requires a separate random secret held by the plugin. D1 stores only its hash. A short-lived server derivation permits delivery retries; the office stores the resulting connection key as a hash. Computer names remain in your account until you remove that connection. Local temporary pairing data is cleared after completion or expiry. Keep the observer folder private to your OS account.

Activity metadata

The local observer sends the coding provider, hashed project/client/session/agent IDs, project display names, generic activity, tool names, timestamps, the machine’s IANA time zone, hashed recipients for recognized handoff/message events and parent links when the client supplies them. Optional task labels, room verticals and briefs are sent only if you put them in your local configuration. Git metadata is read locally to group repositories; remote URLs, credentials and raw folder paths are not sent.

It does not upload prompts, source code, command arguments, tool output or transcripts. Project names and labels may still reveal what you are working on. Choose aliases if that matters. Hashed IDs are not a guarantee of anonymity.

Your office clock

Creating an office saves the connecting computer’s IANA time zone, or your browser’s zone if created on the website. An older office without one uses its first observer’s time zone. The owner can change it in Office settings using account sign-in (or the recovery file for an older office). We do not request GPS coordinates. The zone controls the office clock and day/night lighting and is included in public visitor views.

Storage and access

Cloudflare hosts the website and stores each office in its own Durable Object. The current office keeps up to 160 agents, 80 activity entries and 1,024 recent event IDs. Older entries are displaced as new ones arrive. Remaining records have no automatic expiry; use Manage to delete your office.

Servers store hashes of the ingest, viewer and recovery keys. An ingest key can send activity. A viewer key can read it. The recovery key can read, manage public sharing, replace keys and delete the office. The operator controls the hosting infrastructure; private office access is not end-to-end encryption.

Optional public visits

Only the signed-in owner or an existing owner recovery key can open, update or close a visitor link. A public view contains your chosen office name and motto, character names, harnesses, hierarchy, room themes, generic activity states, the office time zone and timestamps. Project names and room descriptions require a separate opt-in. Task labels, tool details, private collaboration events, event history and keys are never included. Public identifiers remain fingerprints, not a guarantee of anonymity.

Anyone with the link can view or save that information without signing in. Closing it blocks future reads and tells connected visitors to clear their view; another browser's neighborhood may take up to 12 seconds to refresh. It cannot undo screenshots or copies already made. Public views have no permission to control your agents.

Your browser and computer

An HttpOnly cookie keeps you signed in for 30 days. Your browser stores the office ID, demo/live choice, decorations, room briefs and saved friend links. Friends are browser-local bookmarks. A neighborhood fetches their public office states every 12 seconds; direct visits use live WebSockets. Social hangouts happen locally and are not sent to a friend or a coding client. The observer saves its connection under ~/.sidequest/ and queues up to 256 events for up to seven days. Pairing may keep a local backup of your previous connection file.

Cloudflare processes network information to serve requests. Signup and recovery requests use short rate limits based on the connecting IP; the app does not save raw IP addresses in office records. Cloudflare's infrastructure policies also apply. The fonts are served directly by this website; no third-party font request is made. The application includes no advertising or analytics SDK.

Stop reporting, replace keys, or delete

Disable the plugin in your coding clients and stop any retry watcher to stop new reports. Open Connect agents → Manage with your recovery file to replace keys or permanently delete stored office state. Deletion does not remove files from your computer; remove local configuration, backups and queues yourself when no longer needed.

For a social-login account, use My account & agents → Sign-in & account → Delete my account and office. This removes its office data, connection hashes, ownership record, provider records and sessions. Backups retained by Cloudflare follow its retention policies. Recover access through your GitHub or GitLab account. Existing recovery keys remain valid after attaching an older office, so keep those files private. Do not submit keys, transcripts or private activity in the public repository.
